   1  — 01-welcome.webp
   Title: Pick what you'll run
   Caption: Choose a model and the GPUs you'll rent. Each box is a setup
   Gonka has a ready-made configuration for; "Whole 8-GPU servers" run that
   setup several times at once and earn that many times more. The number on
   the right is how many hosts run the model right now.

   2  — 02-welcome-needs.webp
   Title: See exactly what your server needs
   Caption: Once you pick a setup, GNOT lists what to rent: the GPUs, a
   dedicated server (never spot — it can be taken back mid-epoch, and then
   that epoch pays nothing), 1,000 GB of disk, memory of 1.5 × the GPUs',
   Linux with an NVIDIA driver for CUDA 13 (or 12.9), the root login and two
   open ports. "What it earns →" shows what that setup earns before you pay
   for anything.

   3  — 03-your-server.webp
   Title: Tell GNOT where the server is
   Caption: A rented server, which GNOT reaches over SSH, or the computer
   you're using.

   4  — 04-connect.webp
   Title: Connect
   Caption: Paste the SSH command your provider shows, or fill in the
   address, user and key. No SSH key yet? GNOT can make one and shows you
   what to paste into your provider's form.

   5  — 05-health-check.webp
   Title: Health check
   Caption: GNOT checks the server against everything Gonka needs. Anything
   with a Fix button is repaired in one click — Docker, the NVIDIA container
   toolkit, the model downloader, even an old NVIDIA driver — and you see
   the exact commands first.

   6  — 06-wallet.webp
   Title: Your wallet
   Caption: GNOT creates your wallet on your own computer. Its 24 recovery
   words are shown once: write them on paper and never share them — they
   are your wallet and your earnings. The wallet needs a little GNK for the
   setup and for the node's network fees (Gonka's guide suggests 10 GNK).

   7  — 07-network.webp
   Title: How the network reaches your node
   Caption: Simple uses your server's IP address; Secure uses your own
   domain with HTTPS. Either way, ports 8000 and 5000 must be open in your
   provider's dashboard.

   8  — 08-model.webp
   Title: The model configuration
   Caption: GNOT shows Gonka's ready-made configurations, marks the ones
   that fit your server, and picks the one you chose on the first step. On
   8 GPUs it runs a 4-GPU setup twice at once.

   9  — 09-review.webp
   Title: Review and write the configuration
   Caption: Exactly what will be written to your server: the model, your
   node's address and the setup. Both settings files can be opened and read
   first, and internal ports are locked down automatically.

   10 — 10-weights.webp
   Title: Download the model
   Caption: The model files are hundreds of GB, so this is the longest
   step. It resumes by itself if interrupted; you can leave it running.

   11 — 11-launch.webp
   Title: Launch and register
   Caption: GNOT runs Gonka's official launch sequence in order: the
   software, the blockchain node, the server's own key, registering your
   host on the network, the permissions (signed on your computer) and the
   full node. The activity log shows every command.

   12 — 12-collateral.webp
   Title: Collateral
   Caption: A small deposit — a fraction of a GNK — that lets your node
   earn in full; without it the network counts only 20% of your GPUs' work.
   It stays yours and can be withdrawn later; it can be cut by 10–20% for
   downtime or invalid work.

   13 — 13-verify.webp
   Title: Verify
   Caption: GNOT watches your node until it is fully ready — registered,
   reachable from the internet and active — and says what it is doing. You
   can leave it running and walk away.

   14 — 14-finished.webp
   Title: Your node is live
   Caption: Your node is now in My nodes in Gonka Host Monitor/Vote, inside
   the same app: see how it is doing, vote with it, use a command line on
   its server, or shut it down safely. From here: keep the server on, keep
   your 24 words safe, and never delete the .tmkms folder on the server.
