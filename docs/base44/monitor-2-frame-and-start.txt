PROMPT FOR BASE44 — Monitor/Vote, part 2 of 6: the section, its frame and start page
(attach the picture m01-start.png from the folder "monitor-reference-pictures",
then paste everything below and publish)

Add a new section to the site, "Monitor/Vote": the website version of Gonka
Host Monitor/Vote from the GNOT app. Everything in it is public information,
read live from the Gonka network through the backend function gnot-monitor
(already created). The attached picture is the app's start page. It is a
reference for the layout only — do not put the picture on the site. Build it
in the site's own style: the same dark look, fonts and card style as the
other pages.

The website shows the network, any node and every proposal, exactly as the
app does. It does NOT do what needs a node's wallet or its server: linking a
wallet, voting, the command line on a server, shutting a node down. Those
stay in the app; on the website each of them opens the "Needs GNOT" window
(part 5 below).

1. MENU. Add "Monitor/Vote" to the top menu and the phone menu, between
   "Mine or buy" and "FAQ", linking to /monitor. Add it to the footer's
   links in the same place.

2. PAGES:
     /monitor                   the start page (this prompt)
     /monitor/network           The network (next prompt)
     /monitor/node/<address>    one node's page (prompt 4)
     /monitor/votes             Votes (prompt 5)
     /monitor/my-nodes          My nodes (prompt 6)
   Until a page is built, it shows the frame (part 4) and one line: "This
   page is on its way."

3. READING THE DATA. Call the backend function gnot-monitor with a JSON body:
   - { "view": "network" }  — the network's figures. The start page, the
     epoch strip and several pages use it.
   - { "view": "votes" }    — every proposal and the network's shared money.
   - { "view": "node", "address": "gonka1…", "epochs": 30 } — one node.
   - { "view": "vote", "id": 12, "address": "gonka1…" } — how an address
     voted on an open proposal: { "vote": "YES" | "NO" | "NO_WITH_VETO" |
     "ABSTAIN" | null }.
   An answer with an "error" field means the network could not be read:
   show "Couldn't read the network: {error}" in a warning box where the
   figures go, never a blank page. While a page reads, show the site's
   existing loading animation (as on Mine or buy) with "Reading the
   network…". Read again every 2 minutes while a page is open, without
   jumping the scroll or clearing anything typed. Share one reading between
   the pages (moving between them does not read again if the last reading
   is under 2 minutes old).

4. THE FRAME ON EVERY MONITOR PAGE, under the site's header (see the picture):
   a. THE BAR:
      - on /monitor: the title "Gonka Host Monitor/Vote" on the left, a quiet
        "Refresh" button on the right;
      - on every other monitor page: a back button on the left that says
        where it goes ("← Monitor/Vote"; on a node's page "← The network" or
        "← My nodes", whichever it was opened from), three tabs in the middle
        — My nodes · The network · Votes — the current one highlighted (a
        node's page highlights the tab it was opened from), and "Refresh" on
        the right. Refresh reads the page's figures again at once.
   b. THE EPOCH STRIP, under the bar, from the network answer's "epoch":
      - "Epoch {index}" in bold;
      - a small pill: "Serving inference" with a green dot, or "Proof of
        Compute now" with an amber dot when phase contains "poc" (any case);
      - a thin progress bar filled to "progress" (0 to 1), and the
        percentage with one decimal ("92.8%");
      - on the right: "Next Proof of Compute 1h 38m 40s · Mon, 5:48 PM EDT":
        the countdown in bold, ticking every second down from
        secondsToNextPoc (counted from when it was read), then the clock
        time it reaches in the visitor's own time zone (weekday, time, zone
        name). At zero: "Proof of Compute is starting".
      On phones the strip wraps onto two lines.

5. THE "NEEDS GNOT" WINDOW. One window used wherever the app would act with
   a node's wallet or its server: a dialog over a dark backdrop that closes
   with Esc, the × or a click outside. It shows the GNOT logo mark, a title,
   the text, a filled "Download GNOT" button (to /download) and a quiet
   "Not now". The words depend on what opened it:
   - Vote, "Link your wallet", or any Yes / No / No, with veto / Abstain button:
       Title: Voting happens in GNOT
       Text:  A vote is signed with your node's wallet, and that wallet stays
              on your own computer. No website should ever ask for your 24
              words or your wallet's passphrase, so this one doesn't: open
              Monitor/Vote in GNOT, link your node's wallet once, and vote
              there.
   - "Your server":
       Title: The command line is in GNOT
       Text:  GNOT opens a command line on your server from your own
              computer, with your server's login. This website never asks
              for that login.
   - "Shut down safely":
       Title: Shutting down is in GNOT
       Text:  GNOT takes your node off the network the way Gonka's guide
              says to, step by step on your server, with your server's
              login. This website never asks for that login.
   Under the text, small and grey, in every case: "GNOT is the app this
   website is about: it sets up a Gonka node, and it has this same
   Monitor/Vote with voting and your server included."

6. THE START PAGE (/monitor), as in the picture:
   - a small grey eyebrow "GNOT", the title "Gonka Host Monitor/Vote" and
     the line "Follow your nodes, see the whole network, and vote on what it
     decides.";
   - three big cards side by side (stacked on phones). Each has an icon in a
     rounded square, a title, a sentence, a big figure, a small grey line
     under the figure, and an arrow; the whole card is a link:
     • My nodes — "How your nodes are doing, what they earn, and what you
       can do with them." Figure: "Add your node" when this browser keeps no
       nodes, otherwise "1 node" or "{n} nodes". Grey line: "Follow it, vote
       with it, reach its server" when none, otherwise "{n} in this epoch"
       (how many of them are among the network answer's hosts).
       → /monitor/my-nodes
     • The network — "Every host, its GPUs and what it is paid, epoch by
       epoch." Figure: "{n} hosts" (the number of hosts). Grey line:
       "{gpus.total} GPUs · epoch {epoch.index}". → /monitor/network
     • Votes — "What the network is deciding, what each proposal asks for,
       and your vote." Figure: "No open votes", "1 open vote" or "{n} open
       votes" (proposals whose status is VOTING_PERIOD). Grey line: "{n}
       proposals so far". → /monitor/votes
     While a figure is still being read, a small loading animation stands
     in its place.
   - under the cards, small, grey and centred: "Public information from the
     Gonka network. No key, no password." and on a second line: "Voting, the
     command line and shutting down a node are in the GNOT app.", with "the
     GNOT app" linking to /download.
   The nodes "this browser keeps" are a list in the browser's localStorage
   under the key "gnot-monitor-nodes": a JSON list of { "address", "name" }.
   Nothing else uses it yet (prompt 6 fills it); read it safely (an empty
   list when it is missing or unreadable).

7. NUMBERS, written the way the app writes them, everywhere in Monitor/Vote:
   - Amounts arrive in ngonka: 1 GNK = 1,000,000,000 ngonka.
   - GNK in full, with thousands separators: no decimals from 1,000 GNK up,
     2 decimals below 1,000, 3 below 10, 6 below 0.01. "36,394 GNK",
     "0.052 GNK".
   - GNK short: one decimal with k and M, two with B, the plain number below
     10,000: "452.7M GNK", "187.3k GNK".
   - Dollars: "≈ $" and the short form of GNK × price.usd ("≈ $33k"). The
     price comes with the network and node answers (price.usd); leave the
     dollars out when there is none.
   - Percentages: one decimal unless a part says otherwise ("3.6%").
   - Dates: "Oct 16, 2026"; chart days "Oct 16"; full times with the
     visitor's time zone ("Oct 23, 2026, 3:12 PM EDT").
   - Addresses, where short: the first 10 characters, "…", the last 6
     ("gonka1sk6f…7pfvyv"). In full on a node's own page.
   - Model names: only the part after the last "/" ("MiniMax-M2.7").

8. Add the new words to every language the site offers, the same way as on
   the other pages.

Change nothing else on the site.
